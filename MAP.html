2 · Life of one trade

1Call posted"BTO IWM 294C 0dte @0.18" in a room (or an embed, image, or spoken).
2Readcontent.js sees it once; parser makes a signal; if the regex shrugs, the AI reader (Claude) returns a clean call.
3GuardsRoom TEST/REAL · entry vs add/trim/exit · exit policy (ratchet + emergency only) · echo guard (same contract within 20s) · buying power · pocket/pullback rules.
4Order/order → one Webull group: BUY at the bid + STOP leg born with it. Bid rests 90s; no seller → pulled ("NOFILL").
5FilledBook marks FILLED, stop rests at the broker, watchdog thread starts, quote bus watches the contract, underlying price stamped.
6ManagedRatchet walks the stop up by tier; replace-first, never loosens; breached-stop rule; anti-clip. Room trims/stop-moves ignored; FULL exits fire.
7Out & writtenStop hit / ratchet / emergency / room "all out" → SOLD · CLOSED. P&L to trades.log, days/*.json, journal.

4 · Rules that never change

  • Real money is yours alone — placing/cancelling orders by hand, flipping a room LIVE, funding, keys, passwords. Claude never does these.
  • Exits: the ratchet and the emergency-out manage every bot trade. Nobody's trims or stop-moves are followed; a room's FULL exit still fires.
  • Your own hand trades are adopted as visible, never auto-stopped (open question: keep them closeable on a room's "all out", or untouchable?). Positions bigger than bot size are always left alone.
  • Check before redoing: keep a resting stop, remember the winning call, ask the stream before HTTP — fewer calls, fewer naked moments.
  • Never commit settings.json, install the Webull streaming-SDK family into the bridge's Python, or run git from the sandbox (AUTO PUSH owns git).
